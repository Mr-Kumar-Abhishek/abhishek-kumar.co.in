Grade 11 Physical Education (CBSE 2025-26)

Chapter 10: Training & Doping in Sports

10.1 Concept and Principles of Sports Training

Sports Training is a pedagogical process based on scientific principles aiming at preparing athletes for higher performances in sports competitions.

Key Principles of Sports Training

  1. Principle of Continuity: Training must be a continuous, year-round process. Long breaks cause a decline in adaptations.
  2. Principle of Overload: For adaptation to occur and fitness to improve, the training load must exceed the body’s normal threshold.
  3. Principle of Specificity: The body adapts specifically to the type of demand placed on it (e.g., a marathon runner must train by running long distances, not lifting maximum weights).
  4. Principle of Individual Differences: Every athlete responds differently to identical training loads due to genetics, age, and background.
  5. Principle of Rest and Recovery: Adaptation physically occurs during the rest period following the overload. Without recovery, overtraining and injury occur.

10.2 Training Load: Overload, Adaptation, and Recovery

The relationship between load, fatigue, and recovery defines the Supercompensation Cycle.

  • Training Load: The physiological and psychological demand placed on an organism during exercise.
  • Overload: Applying a stimulus greater than what the body is accustomed to. This immediately induces fatigue and a temporary drop in performance.
  • Recovery: The period of rest and nutrition following the overload.
  • Adaptation (Supercompensation): Because the body was stressed, it repairs itself during recovery to be stronger/fitter than its baseline level to handle the stress better next time.

10.3 Warming-Up & Limbering Down

Warming-Up

A preparation period consisting of light physical activity prior to a strenuous workout.

  • Types:
    • General Warm-up: Jogging, stretching, calisthenics (raises core temperature).
    • Specific Warm-up: Rehearsing the actual movements of the sport (e.g., a bowler rolling down a few deliveries in cricket).
  • Importance: Increases blood flow to muscles, reduces blood viscosity, lubricates joints, and exponentially decreases the risk of muscle tears.

Limbering Down (Cooling Down)

A period of light exercise and static stretching immediately following an intense workout.

  • Importance: Slowly returns the heart rate to normal, helps flush out lactic acid from the muscles (reducing delayed onset muscle soreness), and prevents blood pooling in the extremities.

10.4 Concept of Skill, Technique, Tactics & Strategies

Understanding the hierarchy of athletic performance:

  1. Skill: An automatized motor action capable of solving a specific movement task effectively. It is the overall ability an athlete acquires (e.g., passing a football).
  2. Technique: The single, scientifically sound, biomechanical method of executing a physical movement (e.g., striking the football with the inside of the foot while locking the ankle).
  3. Tactics: Specific, short-term plans implemented during the game to overcome a specific defensive or offensive setup of the opponent (e.g., exploiting a slow defender on the right wing).
  4. Strategy: The overarching, long-term master plan decided before the competition begins based on a deep analysis of one’s own team and the opponent.

10.5 Concept of Doping and its Disadvantages

What is Doping?

The World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA) defines doping as the occurrence of one or more anti-doping rule violations, primarily the use of artificial, banned substances or methods (like blood transfusions) to illegally enhance athletic performance.

Common Banned Substances

  • Anabolic Steroids: Used to build muscle mass and strength quickly (causes liver damage, severe aggression/mood swings).
  • Stimulants: Used to increase alertness and reduce fatigue (causes heart attacks, insomnia).
  • Diuretics: Used primarily to mask the presence of other drugs or for rapid weight loss in combat sports (causes severe dehydration, kidney failure).
  • Peptide Hormones (EPO): Increases red blood cell count to boost aerobic capacity (causes blood thickening, strokes).

Disadvantages of Doping

  1. Health Risks: Most banned substances carry extreme, often lethal, side effects (cardiac arrest, liver cancer, irreversible hormonal changes).
  2. Unethical: Completely destroys the core Olympic value of fair play and honest competition.
  3. Career Destruction: Athletes caught doping face public humiliation, stripping of past medals, and multi-year or lifetime bans from their sport.

Competency-Based Questions

Q1. Multiple Choice: Examine the Supercompensation Cycle. What happens biomechanically to the athlete's body if they apply a new, intense training overload *before* the 'Recovery' phase is allowed to fully complete?
  • a) The Supercompensation peak will be doubled.
  • b) The athlete enters a state of overtraining, leading to a steady decline in baseline performance and injury.
  • c) The baseline remains completely unchanged.
  • d) The athlete achieves tactical perfection in their sport.
Answer: (b) The athlete enters a state of overtraining, leading to a steady decline in baseline performance and injury.
Q2. Assertion and Reason:

Assertion (A): After running a 10km marathon, an athlete should immediately stop and sit down to let their heart rest completely.
Reason (R): Limbering down (cooling down) through slow jogging and stretching is scientifically critical to flush out lactic acid and prevent blood pooling in the lower extremities.
  • a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
  • b) Both A and R are true, but R is NOT the correct explanation of A.
  • c) A is true, but R is false.
  • d) A is false, but R is true.
Answer: (d) A is false, but R is true.

Assertion A is false because stopping abruptly causes blood to pool in the legs which can lead to fainting. The Reason R accurately dictates exactly what the athlete *should* do instead.
Q3. Short Answer (2 Marks): Outline the relationship established by the 'Principle of Specificity' in Sports Training. Provide an example.
Answer:
The Principle of Specificity dictates that physiological adaptations are specific to the type of training load applied.
Example: A 100m sprinter must base their training around short, explosive weightlifting routines and maximal sprinting intervals. Forcing them to jog for 15km every day will build slow-twitch endurance, completely contradicting the specific explosive power they need for their sport.
Q4. Short Answer (3 Marks): Briefly distinguish between 'Strategy' and 'Tactics' in sports performance. Using a football match as an example, illustrate both concepts clearly.
Answer:
Strategy: The overarching, pre-planned blueprint decided *before* the match to defeat the opponent over the entire duration.
Tactics: The specific maneuvers and immediate decisions made *during* the match to execute the strategy and adapt to live situations.

Football Example: The coach's strategy is to play heavily defensive and rely entirely on quick counter-attacks because the opposing team is faster but weaker defensively. A tactic to achieve this strategy is assigning two players to permanently double-team the opponent's star midfielder every time he touches the ball.
Q5. Long Answer / Case-Based (5 Marks):

During the National Junior Weightlifting Championships, a 17-year-old athlete sets a new national record, lifting unprecedented weights. However, a post-match random urine test reveals extreme traces of Anabolic Steroids and Diuretics.

1. Define what Doping is under WADA guidelines.
2. Explain the probable reasons why this specific athlete illegally consumed both Anabolic Steroids and Diuretics simultaneously in the context of their sport.
3. Discuss the severe disadvantages and consequences this athlete will face now that they are caught.
Answer:
1. Definition: Doping is the illegal use of artificial, banned substances/methods by an athlete to artificially enhance their physical and mental performance in a competition.

2. Reason for Substances:
- Anabolic Steroids: The fundamental necessity for a weightlifter is extreme muscular strength and mass. Steroids artificially stimulate intense, rapid muscle growth and allow for faster recovery from brutal training sessions.
- Diuretics: Weightlifting is categorized strictly by body-weight classes. Diuretics force the body to rapidly excrete water (urine). The athlete likely took them right before the weigh-in to artificially and dangerously drop 2-3 kilograms of water weight to qualify for a lighter weight class where they would have a massive strength advantage. They are also used to flush out and 'mask' the traces of the steroids in urine tests.

3. Disadvantages/Consequences:
- Career Ruin: The athlete will face public disgrace, immediately lose their newly set national record, be stripped of the medal, and face a minimum multi-year (or lifetime) ban from the sport.
- Health Risks: The steroids risk permanent liver damage, severe hormonal disruption (especially at 17 years old), and psychological aggression. The diuretics put the athlete at imminent risk of severe dehydration and acute kidney failure during the high exertion of lifting.
